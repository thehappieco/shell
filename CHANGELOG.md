# Changelog of @thehappieco/shell

Published to `github.com/thehappieco/shell` by `scripts/shell-package.sh`, which copies this file and refuses a version it does not name.

## 1.6.2 (2026-11-05)

- **A host may reach an app's screens through the console.** New `ThcShell` property `screensThroughConsole` (and `ShellInput.screensThroughConsole` for `buildView`), `false` by default. With it, an in-shell app's screen tabs and the workspace selector's link that keeps a screen name the console's hop path (`/workspaces/{workspace}/apps/{app}/{screen}`) instead of the app's screen: the console then draws the app's settings itself when it keeps them (decision 0054, the settings part), and goes on to the app's screen otherwise. Only the console passes it; an app passes nothing, and its tabs are unchanged. `/v1/shell`'s answer does not change.
- **A held press is told by its event.** `holdNextPress` let a second click with the first one's very time through as if it were that click: a person's double-click never has two equal times, but a test tool's may (Playwright's default `dblclick()`). It now lets only that same event through. Wappie found it in its own suites (2026-10-27).
- **The theme toggle toggles once on a double-click.** `ThcThemeToggle`'s button had no guard of its own, so a double-click moved the theme two steps. It now follows the language menu's button: a click that continues the pointer's press before (`soonAfter`: under `SOON_MS`, within `NEAR_PX`, whatever its `detail`, since Firefox and WebKit give both clicks of a touch double tap `detail` 1) asks for nothing; a click from a key (Enter, Space: `detail` 0), a deliberate click once that moment has passed and a quick click somewhere else each act. Where no crossfade runs (reduced motion, an app without `lookChanger`), the second click lands on the toggle and asks for nothing; in Safari, which focuses no button on a press, it puts focus back on the toggle when the first left it there (the avatar's menu), so the menu's keys go on working. Where the look's crossfade runs (the console and id.), the second press lands on `<html>` in every engine and never reaches the toggle: the theme moves once all the same. The segmented variant needs no guard: a radio asks for the mode it names, never the next one. A test tool's two quick `click()` calls on the toggle (Playwright's land about 30 ms apart, on the same spot) are a double-click too: an app's suite that steps the theme twice waits `SOON_MS` between them, or presses a key. Mailie found it in its review of 1.6.1 (2026-11-04).
- **A press during the look's crossfade closes no avatar menu.** While the crossfade (1.5.0) runs, the browser hit-tests its picture, so every press lands on `<html>` in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit. The avatar's menu took the second press of a double-click on its theme toggle for a press outside it: it closed, and its mousedown left focus on the page. Its document listeners (now `click`, `pointerdown` and `mousedown`, still only while it is open) let such a press through: one on `<html>` while `LOOK_CHANGING` is on it closes nothing, and its mousedown moves no focus, so the menu stays open with focus on the toggle and its keys working. Once the crossfade has ended, a press on `<html>` is a press outside again. Found in the review of Mailie's fixes (2026-11-04).
- **A Tab out of a frame brings the rings back.** The keys pressed in a frame (Mailie's message, in a sandboxed `srcdoc` frame) never reach the document `trackInput` follows, so after a press here, a click into the frame and a Tab out of it, `thc-shell-input-pointer` stayed on `<html>` and the control Tab reached showed no ring. No engine tells the document that a click moved focus into a frame. What reaches it, as the engines driven for this release sent it (Google Chrome 156; Playwright's Chromium, with and without site isolation forced, Firefox and WebKit): the pointer goes onto the frame (a `mouseover` on the frame's own element, for a mouse and a finger alike) where the engine draws the frame in the page's process, which Chrome does not for a cross-site or sandboxed frame (its site isolation), but for the frame's border; what had focus here, if anything had, loses it, in Chromium and WebKit at the click (a `focusout` with no `relatedTarget`, while the body is `document.activeElement`), in Firefox only when focus next moves, at the first Tab in the frame (a `focusout` while the frame's element is `document.activeElement`, which names the control Tab reaches here when the frame holds nothing focusable, such as a plain-text message); and the Tab out focuses a control here that the browser marks `:focus-visible`, with no `relatedTarget` but after that Firefox `focusout`, whose `focusin` names the control that lost focus. Such a `focusin` now takes the class off when all three hold. First, focus was in a frame, by one of these signs: a `focusout` while a frame's element (an `iframe`, `frame`, `object` or `embed` of the document) is `document.activeElement`, with no `pointerdown`, `pointerup`, `click` or `focusin` here since, which counts whatever the `focusin`'s `relatedTarget`; or, for a `focusin` with no `relatedTarget` only, a `mouseover` on a frame's element since the latest `pointerdown` here (or that `pointerdown` on one, a press on its border), or, with no `pointerdown`, `pointerup`, `click` or `focusin` here since, a `focusout` with no `relatedTarget` while `document.hasFocus()` is true, of a control that could then still hold focus as it was and that is still in the page when focus comes back. A control could still hold focus when it is in the page, enabled, not inert, drawn (`checkVisibility()` asked for the `visibility` property under both its names, `visibilityProperty` and `checkVisibilityCSS`, else a box) and focusable: by a `tabindex` whose value is an integer (`tabindex="x"`, or the empty value Vue writes for a bound empty string, is none), by being editable (an editing host, such as a reply composer, whose `tabIndex` reads -1 in every engine), by being a `dialog`, by being an `option` (a customizable select's picker, `appearance: base-select`, focuses one), by being a scroller that scrolls (`overflow` `auto` or `scroll` with more to scroll, which Chromium and Firefox let the keyboard reach with nothing focusable in it), or by a `tabIndex` of 0 or more (a kind focusable by default; a link or an area only with an `href`). Inert is what Chromium computes (CSS's `interactivity`), through every shadow root; elsewhere, the `inert` attribute on the control or an element it is drawn in, up through an open shadow root's slot and each shadow host. Inside an open shadow root the control is the one the event's composed path starts at (a web component's button, which the event's target names by its host), inside a closed one its host, judged as itself (a custom element is focusable only by a `tabindex` of its own). Chromium also takes focus to nowhere from a control a script disables, makes inert (as the shell's workspace panel turns while it fades) or hides (`display`, `visibility`, or `content-visibility` on an ancestor), from one that loses its `tabindex`, its `href`, its being editable or its focusability by default (a `summary` another one displaces), and, just before it goes, from one the page takes out, and the other engines from some of these: none of those counts, but where a closed shadow root hides it (below), nor does a press's own blur, which comes before that press's release or click, or, for a press that becomes a native drag (a draggable element or image, a link), which sends neither, before its `dragstart` (Chromium and Firefox also send a `pointercancel`, WebKit only the `dragstart`). Second, it comes more than `PRESS_FOCUS_MS` (new export, 1000 ms) after the latest `pointerdown`, `pointerup` or `click` here, each counted from when the document handled it (the later of its `timeStamp` and `performance.now()` then, since Chromium and WebKit stamp a press when the system saw it, so a press a busy main thread held back would look older than it is). A press's own focus comes well within that bound: on the press, or once a menu or dialog has run its motion (the language dialog gives focus back about 200 ms after the click that closed it); a modal dialog a click opens takes focus at once, which Safari rings. Whether the press moved focus does not matter: a second press on what already has focus, a press whose `mousedown` is held back (`holdNextPress`, a menu's head, the crossfade's) and a press outside anything focusable each count. Third, the browser rings the control it lands on (inside an open shadow root the control itself: the engines mark a web component's button `:focus-visible`, never its host). A focus the page moves itself long after a press, with no sign (the console's error notice after a slow failure, an app's heading after a slow route, a notice or a text field after the control pressed was disabled, made inert, hidden or taken out, or lost its `tabindex`, its `href`, its being editable or its focusability by default, also inside an open shadow root or drawn in one's slot, the heading after an entry of the workspace panel was clicked and the route took over a second) leaves the class on, as in 1.6.1, though each engine marks some of those `:focus-visible`: the rings stay hidden, a text field's aside, which `tokens.css` never hides. `trackInput` adds seven passive, capture-phase listeners on the document to its `pointerdown` and `keydown` (`pointerup`, `click`, `pointercancel`, `dragstart`, `mouseover`, `focusout`, `focusin`), keeps a number, two flags and a reference per document, runs no timer and stores nothing; it reads the DOM's own properties as it walks up from the control that lost focus, so an element a page names after one (`<input name="parentNode">` is its form's `parentNode`) cannot send the walk round. The limits. A Tab out of a frame within `PRESS_FOCUS_MS` of the latest press here (a press, a click into the frame and a Tab out of it, all within a second) is taken for that press's own focus, so the control it lands on shows no ring and the rings stay hidden until the next key pressed in this document. Focus that reached a frame with no sign brings no ring back on its way out either, until the next key here: in Chrome, a click into a frame of its own process while nothing here has focus (after a press on text outside anything focusable: the shell's main area takes focus at a press in it that focuses nothing else and whose `mousedown` no script prevents, but a page's body and the shell's side column do not), or from a control in a closed shadow root whose host has no `tabindex` (the `focusout` names the host); in Chromium and WebKit, focus a script puts on the frame's element (its `focus()`); and a frame inside a shadow root gives neither of a frame's own signs, since the `mouseover` and `document.activeElement` name the shadow's host, so only a control here that lost focus to it with no `relatedTarget` is one (in Firefox, a plain-text frame in a shadow root showed no ring). And once a sign has come, a focus the page itself moves more than `PRESS_FOCUS_MS` after the latest press, onto an element the browser rings, can pass for a Tab out: it rings, and the rings show wherever the browser puts them until the next press here. After Firefox's sign, that is such a focus from anywhere, the page moving focus out of the frame; after the pointer's or a control's, only one with no `relatedTarget`, since a focus the page moves from a control here keeps the class. The pointer's sign lasts until the next `pointerdown`, though the page has moved focus back out of the frame meanwhile. Some signs come with no person in a frame: the pointer crossing a frame on its way across the page, where the engine draws the frame in the page's process; a script putting focus into a frame (in Chromium and WebKit, by its `contentWindow.focus()`, which takes focus from the control here to nowhere; in Firefox, by that or by the frame's element's `focus()`, after which its `focusout` comes at the next move); a script's `blur()` of the control that has focus; in Chromium, the page moving that control within the page (taken out and put back, as a keyed list reorders), which Chromium blurs as it goes and which is back when focus comes (Firefox and WebKit sent no `focusout` for such a move); and what a closed shadow root hides from the document: a control in one whose host has a `tabindex` of its own, which the page disables, makes inert, hides or takes out while the host stays (in Chromium, and in Firefox but for one taken out, for which it sent no `focusout`; WebKit kept focus on the host), and, in Firefox, which computes no `interactivity`, a control drawn in such a root's slot whose wrapper there the page makes inert (WebKit took focus from the button at its press). Firefox with its own site isolation (Fission) and Safari could not be driven here, and no headless engine lets a window lose focus. Mailie found it in its review of 1.6.1 (2026-11-04); the reviews of the fix found that Chrome's site isolation hid the pointer from it, that Firefox brings focus out of a frame with nothing focusable in it with a `relatedTarget`, that a control made inert or hidden passed for focus gone into a frame, that an editing host, a dialog and a web component's button, whose `tabIndex` reads -1 though a press focuses them, did not; that a page naming an element `host` hung the walk, while a control in a shadow root that focus reached unheard, one in a closed root, and one drawn in a slot whose wrapper went inert passed for focus gone into a frame; and that a customizable select's option and a scroller did not, while a drag's own blur and a control whose `tabindex` stopped being an integer did.
- **For an app:** on a page of your own with a frame a person may Tab out of (Mailie's message), let the area around the frame take focus at a press, as the shell's main area does (`tabindex="-1"`). In Chrome, which draws a sandboxed or cross-site frame in a process of its own, the focus that area, or the control pressed, loses to the frame is the only sign the page gets: after a press on text outside anything focusable, a Tab out of such a frame showed no ring in Chrome 156 until the next key, and with the area focusable it rang. In the shell, a press in its main area gives focus to the area or to the control pressed, unless a script prevents its `mousedown`, and that is enough, but for a control in a closed shadow root whose host has no `tabindex`; after a press on the side column's text, Chrome showed no ring, as on a bare page. A web component a person may press before such a frame should use an open shadow root.

## 1.6.1 (2026-10-26)

The owner's requests on 1.6.0: no outline on another button after a click, and smoother, slower motion with no double toggling. Additive for a host's code: every 1.6.0 call still builds.

- **A focus ring after a key only.** New export `trackInput(doc?)` (`input.ts`, with `POINTER_INPUT`): one passive, capture-phase `pointerdown` and `keydown` listener pair on the document, added once, puts `thc-shell-input-pointer` on `<html>` after a press of a mouse, pen or finger, and takes it off at any key that is not a modifier alone. `tokens.css` then hides every focus ring while the class is there (`:root.thc-shell-input-pointer :focus-visible:not(input, textarea, select, [contenteditable])`, the browser's own ring included), and the shell's focus tints (the avatar's items, "Sair", the menus' toggle and flag, the language menu's items) follow `:focus-visible` from the keyboard only. Focus moves exactly as before, so a keyboard user loses nothing. `ThcShell` calls it when it mounts; a host's pages outside the shell call it once at boot (the console and id. do). Why: Safari draws a ring where a modal dialog puts focus when it opens and gives it back when it closes, after a click as after a key, and the next focus a script moves then rings too (the drawer's close button, the menu button, the language's options).
- **Motion.** New tokens in `tokens.css`: `--thc-ease` (thehappie.co's `cubic-bezier(0.22, 0.61, 0.36, 1)`), `--thc-motion-open` (240 ms) and `--thc-motion-close` (180 ms). The avatar's menu and the workspace selector fade and drop in and out (a `<Transition name="thc-shell-pop">`); the drawer slides in from the left over a fading backdrop and slides out before it closes; the language's dialog rises and fades in and fades out; the chevrons turn; the header's language menu and the toggle's icon take the same timing. A dialog closes once its exit has run: new export `whenStill(el, then)` (`motion.ts`) waits for the browser's own animations, with no timer. A link followed from the drawer closes it at once, so the next page never waits behind it. Nothing moves under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **No double toggling.** A press of the pointer that continues the one before (under 300 ms after it and within 24 px of where it landed: the second of a double-click, or a quick second tap) is no new request: new exports `soonAfter(event, press)`, `pressedAt(event)` (a `Press`: the click's time and place), `SOON_MS` and `NEAR_PX` (`input.ts`), which read the events' own times and places (a pointer's press or release counts whatever its `detail`; a click only with `detail` above 0), so a deliberate press after that moment always acts, however slow the system's double-click speed, a quick press somewhere else is a new request, and a click from a key or assistive technology (`detail` 0) is never held back. On the avatar, the header's language menu and the workspace selector, the double-click's second press is ignored (and in Safari focus goes back into the open menu, so its keys work). Where a drawer or a dialog has just opened under the pointer, the press is held back before it reaches anything (its mousedown moves no focus from where the dialog put it, and in Safari, which would put it on the dialog itself, the click puts it back on the language shown): the drawer's brand, a link or its backdrop; a language under the flag on a phone. The same holds where a menu or a dialog has just closed under the pointer: new export `holdNextPress(event, doc?)` holds back, once, the press that continues (soon, on the same spot) a press on an entry that closed its menu (a link, a language, "Sair") or on what closed a dialog (the drawer's and the language dialog's backdrop and close button): its mousedown does nothing (no focus moves, no word is selected), and its click and dblclick reach nothing. A double-click on an entry acts once and never on the page beneath, while a quick press elsewhere, on the page a link has just routed to, is the person's own. A double-click on a link the browser follows keeps the loading mark: its second press is no sign that the browser did not go. And in Safari, which focuses no button on a press, pressing the avatar or the language menu again closed the menu on the press and opened it again on the click: focus going to an ancestor that holds the menu (the page's main area) is no longer taken as leaving it; a press outside, with any button, closes it, and a press on the avatar menu's head or a gap never moves focus from its item, while a press on its links and buttons is theirs (a link still drags, a button takes focus; where Safari gives the theme toggle none, the toggle takes it back, so the menu's keys go on working).
- **Keyboard focus keeps what it had.** The focus tints are wrapped in `:where()`, so a current item or language keeps its tone when focused, as before; the workspace selector's list is `inert` as soon as it starts closing, so Tab never lands in a list that is fading away.
- **For an app:** take 1.6.1, and call `trackInput()` once at your boot for pages outside the shell; your own focus rules then follow the pointer the same way, since `tokens.css` holds the rule. Your own menus may use the motion tokens and `whenStill`, and `soonAfter`, `pressedAt` and `holdNextPress` against a double toggle.

## 1.5.0 (2026-10-23)

The look's crossfade, and links without the look (decision 0052; contract 3.3). Additive for a host's code: every 1.4.1 call still builds.

- **New exports: `lookChanger(shown, doc?)` and `changeLook(update, doc?)`** (`motion.ts`, with `LOOK_CHANGING` and the types `LookChanger`, `LookDocument` and `LookValues`). `changeLook` is the crossfade: the browser's View Transitions API takes a picture of the page, `update` runs, and the picture fades into the new page over 0.3 s with thehappie.co's easing (`cubic-bezier(0.22, 0.61, 0.36, 1)`). It waits for Vue's next render inside the transition, so a language's new words are in the new picture. It runs `update` exactly once, always: at once, with no crossfade, where the browser has no View Transitions API, under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, on a hidden page and on a page being prerendered. Inside the crossfade `update` runs a frame after the call. It starts no timer, stores nothing and sets no style.
- **Show every change of the look through one `lookChanger`.** `const look = lookChanger(() => ({ locale, theme }))` (what the page shows now), then `look.show({ theme: next }, () => yourApply(next))` for every change: the header's toggle and menu, My account, a save's answer and its refusal's put-back, and the account's look arriving (a session, `/v1/shell`'s answer). A change that differs from what the page shows starts a crossfade; one that does not runs at once, so the page still remembers what it shows. While a crossfade's update has not run yet (the frame the browser takes its picture in), every further change joins that update, after the ones before it: none runs ahead of an older one (a page turned hidden, reduced motion), none is dropped (its writes still happen), and an echo starts no second crossfade. `look.settled()` resolves once every change shown so far has run, before reading the look the page went to. Calling `changeLook` directly and comparing with what is on screen loses a put-back or an account's look that arrives within that frame. Never call either at boot.
- **tokens.css** gains the crossfade's timing, scoped to `:root.thc-look-changing`, the class `changeLook` puts on `<html>` while it runs, so no other view transition of a host takes it; none under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Links carry no look** (decision 0052, which supersedes 0050's links): `consoleHomeURL` is the console's root with no query, and `signInURL` carries no theme (it keeps `locale`, for the console's own error pages). Both still take the 1.4.1 arguments, unused.
- **For an app:** show your theme and language changes (your header's handlers, and where the account's look applies from `/v1/shell`) through one `lookChanger`, and give your first frame the company cookies' look before anything paints: a blocking classic script in `<head>` that sets `data-theme` (the site's `boot.js` pattern), or your server writing `data-theme` and `<meta name="color-scheme">` into the page from the cookies, as id. and the console do.

## 1.4.1 (2026-10-23)

The language and theme travel with the shell's links drawn without the console's data (decision 0050). Additive: a host that passes no `theme` gets the same links as with 1.4.0, plus the language on the console's home.

- **Signed out:** the notice's sign-in link to the console's `/auth/login` carries the host's `theme` beside `locale`, which the console sends on to id.
- **The console not answering, or another account's:** the notice's link and the brand go to the console's home with `?ui_locales=` and, when the host passes one, `&ui_theme=`, which the console shows for that page.
- **New export:** `consoleHomeURL(consoleOrigin, locale, theme?)`. `signInURL` takes an optional fourth argument, the theme.
- **For an app:** pass `theme` to `ThcShell` (the theme the page shows), as the console does. With the console's data, nothing changes: both ends take the account's language and theme from it.

## 1.4.0 (2026-10-19)

Moving between the console and an app without the menu reloading (design `docs/design/2026-10-12/shell-1.4-instant-moves.md`, decision 0047; contract 1.5, 2.4, 3.2 and 3.3). Additive: a host that passes none of the new props draws exactly as with 1.3.0.

- **The kept answer.** `ThcShell` takes `initial` (`ShellData | null`): the last answer of `GET /v1/shell` the host showed, which it keeps itself. While `result` is `null` the shell draws it as it draws data (mode `kept`, `data-shell="kept"`), so a new page has its menu and header in its first frame and only the content area waits; the fresh `result` replaces it in place, under the same brand, and a refusal draws what 1.3.0 draws. It is drawn only when its `console_origin` is the host's `consoleOrigin` and, when `subject` is given, its `sub` is `subject`; a host that passes a `fallback` (an app) gets it drawn only with such a `subject`, that is once its own session is known for the same person. An app passes its fresh `result` only once its own session is known, together with its `subject`: before that the shell cannot tell another person's console from the app's person. It speaks the host's `locale`, and the header's controls show the host's `theme` and `locale`: an app sets its language and theme from the kept answer before passing it.
- **Read-only.** `readOnly` draws the kept answer read-only (the console, until its fresh answer arrives): every link keeps its `href`, but a click follows nothing and shows no loading mark; the workspace selector says `aria-disabled="true"` and does not open; the root has `data-read-only="true"`, and the side column, the drawer's panel and the tabs row `aria-busy="true"`. The theme and the language keep working. A host's own `menu-footer` is the host's to disable.
- **"Sair"'s hook.** `beforeSignOut` runs, synchronously, when the person follows the shell's own "Sair" (not a host's footer, and not while read-only), before the browser follows it: an app erases its kept answer there.
- **New exports**, which touch no storage: `KEPT_SHELL_KEY` (`thehappie-shell.last`, the one key on the console and every app), `MAX_KEPT_TEXT`, `keptText(data)` (the JSON of `parseShell`'s checked copy, or `null`: keep nothing) and `readKept(text, consoleOrigin)` (back through `parseShell` with the host's console origin, or `null`; it never throws); and, for a page the browser prerenders, `isPrerendering`, `whenActivated(fn)` (at once on a shown page, else once at `prerenderingchange`, in order) and `activated()`, with the type `ActivationDocument`.
- **The shell still stores nothing.** The host reads, writes and erases the kept answer (decision 0047's rules: contract 1.5): an app keeps it for its own session's person, erases it at its own sign-out, at "Sair" (`beforeSignOut`), when its session has ended or is another account's than the kept answer's (as soon as the session answers, whichever of the two answers first), and at a console 401 or another account's answer. On a page the browser prerenders it writes nothing of what it read before the person arrived (it asks again then, and keeps that answer), and erases through `whenActivated`.
- **Prerendering is the host's** (contract 2.4): an app's `/console/*` documents name speculation rules for the console's pages by the `Speculation-Rules` header (a JSON file served as `application/speculationrules+json`), opt in with `Supports-Loading-Mode: credentialed-prerender` only when the request's `Referer` origin is the console's, are `Cache-Control: no-store`, and keep every boot side effect behind `whenActivated`, asking again for their session and `GET /v1/shell` when the person arrives.
- Update an app's own tests: the guard that no storage write carries the sub, the name or a workspace allows exactly `KEPT_SHELL_KEY`, written by `keptText`.

## 1.3.0 (2026-10-11)
